# The SI 618 project

The project asks your group to find two or more public datasets that nobody has
combined in quite the way you will, merge them into something more useful than
either one alone, and use it to answer questions you care about. It runs across
the whole term and applies each part of the course in turn: manipulation in
Milestone 1, statistical analysis in Milestone 2, and machine learning in
Milestone 3.

You are not expected to use tools or techniques from outside this course, and you
are discouraged from doing so, because the project is assessed on how well you
apply what the course teaches.

## Forming a group

Groups have two or three members. Working alone is not permitted, and a group of
four or more has to split into two groups.

Forming a group is up to you. The `#project` channel on Slack is the place to say
what you are interested in and to find people who share it, and a shared interest
in a topic is a better basis for a group than a shared table in class.

**Register your group by Monday, October 5, at 11:59 PM**, by joining one of the
groups in the **Project Groups** set under *People* on Canvas, all members joining
the same one. Anyone not in a group by then will be placed in one by the teaching
team, so if you are having trouble finding a group, please say so on Slack or in
office hours before that date rather than after it.

## Choosing data

The best predictor of how well a project goes is whether the group is genuinely
interested in its data. These are good places to start looking:

- [Deep Blue Data](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/data), the University of
  Michigan's research data repository
- [data.gov](https://www.data.gov/), and the open data portals of individual cities
  and states, such as [NYC Open Data](https://opendata.cityofnewyork.us/)
- [World Bank World Development Indicators](https://datatopics.worldbank.org/world-development-indicators/)
- [CDC data](https://data.cdc.gov/) and [HealthData.gov](https://healthdata.gov/)
- [Kaggle datasets](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets), although many are
  already cleaned and merged, which leaves less for Milestone 1 to do
- [awesome-public-datasets](https://github.com/awesomedata/awesome-public-datasets),
  a long list organised by topic

The datasets must be public, so that a grader can obtain them from the source you
cite, and they need a column or combination of columns on which they can be
matched, such as a date, a place, an identifier or a name.

## Submitting

Each milestone is one marimo notebook, submitted by one member on behalf of the
group, as a `.py` file and its HTML export. Please do not upload the data, since
the notebook should load it from its source URL wherever possible, and otherwise
say exactly where each file came from and how to obtain it.

Attribute substantial AI assistance as the syllabus describes, in a sentence saying
what you used and what it did.
